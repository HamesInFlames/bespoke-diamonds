CRM for Bespoke Diamonds

Romi already owns most of a CRM and pays a second vendor to duplicate part of it. The right move is to use what he has properly, wire the new site into it, and step up to jewellery-specific software only when the business outgrows it.

BusinessAppointment-only diamantaire, retail custom work plus wholesale to retailers
Today's stackLeadConnector (GoHighLevel) calendar · Podium texting · Google Ads · website form

01

What a CRM has to do for this business

Bespoke Diamonds is not a jewellery store in the usual sense. It is appointment-only, one diamantaire facing the client, low volume and high ticket, and it runs two businesses at once.

Wholesale

Loose diamonds to retailers such as Birks, European Jewellery and Calgary Jewellery, which means memo and consignment tracking and repeat B2B accounts.

Leads arrive from six places: the website form, the booking calendar, the Podium text bubble, phone calls from Google Ads, Instagram and Facebook messages, and referrals. Today nothing joins them up.

Compliance

  • CASL. Express consent (an unchecked box, recorded with date and source) before any marketing text or email; an unsubscribe link in every marketing message; consent records kept three years. Appointment reminders and order updates are transactional and exempt. Penalties reach $10 million per violation for businesses.
  • PIPEDA. No Canadian data-residency rule, but the privacy policy must disclose processing by US providers, someone (Romi) is the named privacy officer, and the business stays accountable for what vendors do with the data.
02

What Romi already has

The current site embeds a LeadConnector booking calendar. LeadConnector is the white-label name of GoHighLevel when an agency resells it, so Romi already has a GoHighLevel sub-account, almost certainly under Variance Marketing's agency plan. GoHighLevel is a full CRM: contacts, pipelines, calendar, two-way SMS and email, automations, forms, review requests, a website chat widget and a mobile app. He is probably using a fraction of it.

He also pays for Podium for the text-us bubble and review requests. Podium's Core plan lists at $399 a month, and most small businesses land between $450 and $600 after fees and add-ons on an annual contract. Everything Podium does for him, GoHighLevel also does.

First question for Romi: who owns the GoHighLevel account, and what does he pay Variance and Podium each month? The answer changes the recommendation more than any feature comparison.

CBuild a custom CRM

Not recommended as the system of record. A CRM's value is in the messaging, calendar, consent handling and mobile app, which take years to get right. What Kim Consultant should build is the glue: the new site's forms and enquiry buttons post straight into the CRM with the piece, occasion and consent captured. A small private client-and-stone book could live on the site later if a packaged CRM cannot hold the lifetime-upgrade records the way Romi wants.

DWholesale side

Rapaport Trade (RapNet) is the marketplace, not a CRM. Memo and consignment tracking at Romi's volume fits a second pipeline in the CRM (requested → on memo → due → returned or sold) with reminders before memo expiry. Dedicated diamond-supplier software only earns its cost with multi-location inventory and marketplace sync.

04

Recommendation

Phase 1, now: make GoHighLevel the CRM of record and retire Podium.

  1. Confirm ownership. If Variance owns the sub-account, have it transferred or open a Starter account and move the calendar. Romi should own his client list.
  2. Two pipelines. Custom order: enquiry → consult booked → consulted → quoted → deposit → CAD approval → in production → ready → delivered → review requested. Wholesale memo: requested → on memo → due → closed.
  3. Custom fields. Occasion, partner name, proposal or wedding date, anniversary, ring size, metal, budget band, stone cert number, appraisal date and value, upgrade-eligible, CASL consent date and source.
  4. Automations. Booking confirmation and reminder; temporary-ring task at day three; review request seven days after delivery; appraisal reminder at twelve months; anniversary text at eleven months (consent required); dormant-at-180-days nudge to Romi, not the client.
  5. Replace Podium. GoHighLevel's chat widget and SMS number take over the text bubble and Google review requests. Expected saving: $400 to $600 a month.
  6. Site integration (KC, build Phase 3). Contact form and "enquire about this piece" post to a GoHighLevel inbound webhook with piece name and consent flag; booking page passes the selected service; phone clicks keep the Google Ads conversion.
  7. Compliance. Consent checkbox on every form, unchecked by default, wording stored; unsubscribe on every marketing message; privacy policy names US processing; Romi named as privacy contact.
Phase 2, when it hurts

Step up to jewellery software on a trigger, not a hunch.

A second staff member or a wish-list workflow means Clientbook. Inventory, repair tickets and appraisals in one place means Jewel360, which bundles Clientbook. Until one of those is true, the extra $300 or more a month buys nothing he will use.
